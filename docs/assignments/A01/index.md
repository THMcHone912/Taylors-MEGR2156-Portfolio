## Task B: Product Analysis

https://patents.google.com/patent/CN213550770U/en?q=(office+chair)&oq=office+chair
## A. Primary Function 
The primary function of this product is to support a seated human load while maintaining a stable, adjustable seating position and transmitting the user's weight via the seat, support structure and base into the floor. Physically and mechanically, the chair acts as a load-bearing structure. The backrest and seat apply forces to the supporting frame, support structure, weight through the seat and base of the chair into the floor. The design must provide sufficient stiffness, stability and strength to support the applied load, while the central support and base distributes the forces across the floor. The office chair must provide sufficient strength, stability and stiffness to support the applied load without excessive tipping or deformation. 

## B. Governing Model
The primary behavior of the chair can be traced back to engineering mechanics I, or static equilibrium. The behavior can be modeled via these equations: ∑F=0 and ∑M=0. The variables in these equations and in static equilibrium are F, W=mg, m, g, M, and r. F is the applied forces, including the user's weight. W=mg is the weight of the user. m is the user's mass. g is the gravitational acceleration. M is the moment about a selected point. Finally, r is the perpendicular distance from the force to the reference point. The chair must remain in equilibrium while also supporting the user. The location of the user's weight relative to the chair's support structure creates moments that the frame and base must resist. The assumption here is that the chair can be treated as a rigid body under static loading. This means that the user's movement is slow enough that acceleration and dynamic effects can be neglected. This model is appropriate for analyzing the chair while the user is sitting in normal conditions, rather than during sudden movements.

## D. Patent Number and Author(s)
The patent number is CN213550770U, the site referenced for it is here: https://patents.google.com/patent/CN213550770U/en?q=%28office+chair%29&oq=office+chair&utm_source=chatgpt.com
Authors of the office chair include multiple, such as Charles Darwin, Thomas E. Warren, Frank Lloyd Wright, and Bill Stumpf. 
Some alternative designs that have the same primary function are a fixed pedestal chair; this is essentially the same design, just without the rolling wheels. Another alternate design is the four-legged office chair. Instead of the base and central column, the four-leg frame instead transfers the user's weight directly from the seat into four structural legs and then into the floor. 
An important design decision visible in the chair's geometry is the usage of the central support column combined with the wide multi-arm base. The engineer probably chose this configuration to concentrate the vertical load through the center of the chair, while having a wide base to maintain stability. Using a narrow base would make the chair become more susceptible to tipping when the user's center of mass moves away from the centerline. Extending the base outward on each side increase the support footprint, and increases the moment required to tip the chair. At the same time, the column eliminates the need for four separate structural legs because it allows for the seat assembly to be adjusted and supported around a center axis.
